## Revision notes per asset

### instrument-aurora-coil
#### Round-2 verdict: REJECTED. Revision notes (round 3, final slot)
What is good and must be KEPT from `../round-2/out/instrument-aurora-coil.png`: the brass base and cap (matte satin gold, 3 to 4 px ink contour, riveted collar, three feet), the dark matte core, palette, clean alpha (corners and 3 percent border are 0, no fringe on ink-900, parchment, black, magenta; the technical side passed).
What is wrong:
1. Fails the one-line read at 48 px. At 48 px the coil reads as a brown barrel/candle with a thin squiggle on top, not "spring + wavy ribbon". The 10 copper turns are thin hairlines that merge into a brown striped drum (copper reads as shading, not as a wire), and the turns are flat bands slanting steeply with pointed, leaf-like ends at both sides (they look like sharpened pencil strokes, not round wire). The ribbon is the same silhouette problem as round 1: a tapering pastel comma/S-curl that narrows to a hairline at the nozzle and again at the tip, so its visible mass at 48 px is about 6 px wide; it still reads as a smoke wisp. Its colour blend is a smooth teal-grey to rose gradient with little wash structure.
2. The base is a very wide flat slab (about 590 px wide, about 20 percent of the height) with the coil column sitting on a tiny pedestal; the object reads as a candle on a saucer, and the proportion differs from the orrery/beacon bases.
3. The wire start at the bottom (a loop on the pedestal) and end at the top (pointed tip beyond the cap edge at the right) are not brass terminals as specified.
Change:
- Ribbon: make it BOLD. Minimum visible width 70 px along at least 60 percent of its length, maximum 150 px, a ribbon that is a flat folded band with a front face and a clearly differently shaded back face at ONE fold, ends cut as clean tapered shapes (not hairline). Start from a nozzle 30 to 40 px wide (a small brass flared spout) rather than a 6 px thread. Teal base, faint green middle, rose only on the far edge, with visible layered wash edges. It must still read as a ribbon (cloth/silk), not vapour, at 48 px: the test is that at 48 px a viewer sees a distinct teal wave shape about 14 px wide.
- Helix: thicker wire, 32 to 40 px, 7 to 9 turns instead of 10, rounded wire ends and brass terminal blocks at the bottom and top, front strands visibly overlapping a lighter lit-edge so each turn reads as a tube. Wire colour contrast against the dark core must hold in greyscale at 48 px (lit edge #C98558, no brighter).
- Proportions: base about 520 to 580 px wide but about 14 percent of the height (not 20), taller pedestal/collar like the accepted beacon or orrery; coil column 210 to 250 px core diameter; overall bounding box long side 74 to 82 percent of the canvas.
- Match: `accepted/instrument-orrery.png` (base and collar style) and `accepted/instrument-telescope.png` (brass tone).

## Recurring failures: these are MUST-NOTS for every asset here

1. Serrated / polygon-lobed / scalloped / mosaic cells in sky, nebula, cloud or wash edges (Kuwahara or oil-filter look, crumpled stucco grain). Rejected in 4 rounds for lore-1, lore-6, feature-graphic. Wash edges must be soft wet edges, with at most gentle back-run blooms larger than 60 px; no outlined cells, no repeating lobes of similar size. If a nebula or cloud will not come out smooth, leave it out: a plain dark navy wash with paper grain beats a lobed nebula every time.
2. Mirror-symmetric or reflected or tiled composition anywhere (the nebula was mirrored left-right into a butterfly in lore-1 and feature-graphic). No flip, no reflect, no clone-stamp. Check by flipping your result horizontally and differencing against the original: it must not match.
3. Blocky, pixel-stepped or hard-threshold blooms (icon, 3 rounds). Any bloom, glow or wash that was composited from a mask must have a feather of at least 12 px and come from a smooth native wash; never a thresholded or low-resolution alpha. Prefer no bloom to a blocky bloom.
4. "Pasted on" objects: an object whose texture, grain, edge sharpness or colour temperature differs from the surrounding sky (telescope pasted on the dome, building pasted onto the sky, flat slate pasted on a roof, star pasted on the sky). Every object must share the same paper grain and the same ink-contour weight as accepted assets and must have contact (feet, base, shadow gap) with what it sits on.
5. Clip-art stars: faceted two-tone eight-point vector stars with ruler-straight edges. Stars here are hand-painted gouache dabs (see the star spec in the icon section), never faceted foil halves, never discs with halos (a disc reads as a moon, which is forbidden).
6. Off-palette loudness: hot orange copper (#F97A34, #FF7F1F), saturated cyan glass, lime-yellow, coral, violet, glossy white specular streaks, candy gradients. Maximum 5 hue families, low-to-mid saturation, matte (art bible sections 3, 4, 12.7).
7. Objects that fail their one-line read at display size (a key that is a tiny ring, a ribbon 18 px wide at 48 px, a telescope lost under cloth, a star hidden by the tube).
8. Edit-pass contamination: relighting, recolouring or restyling a whole accepted image by edit prompts produced the stucco and lobed textures twice (lore-6). Mechanical crop, resize, placement and feathered compositing of accepted pixels are allowed; full-image restyle edits are not. Generate each sky in a single fresh native pass (at 1.5x to 2x size, then Lanczos downscale is fine). No blur, noise or filter overlay to disguise texture.
