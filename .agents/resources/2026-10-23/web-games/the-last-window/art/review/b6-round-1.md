# Review: The Last Window, batch b6, round 1

Technical (all ten): 1024x1024 RGBA PNG, corners and outer 3 percent border alpha 0, silhouettes opaque, soft contact shadow max alpha below 250 only outside the body, no baked checkerboard, edges clean on night, nightMid, paper and wood (see d-books.png, d-decor.png). Technical is not the problem this round; style and construction are.

Contact sheets in this folder: a-books, a-decor (full size), b-display, b-display2 (32/48/64/96/128 px), c-books-vs-accepted, c-decor-vs-accepted, d-books, d-decor (four backgrounds), crop-*.png (detail crops).

Context: accepted drink-6 was rejected in b5 round 1 for a diagonal lattice / quilt texture. The same defect is applied here as the bar: repeated diagonal diamond tiles on flat areas is a hard reject; broad continuous brush passes are accepted.

## book-1 ACCEPTED
Forest-green cloth hardback, thickest of the set, two gold spine bands, lamp emblem with exactly five rays, no text. Pose, pitch, page-block band and key-light-left match the accepted drinks. Brush passes are broad and continuous, no lattice. Emblem is small but reads as a lamp at 96 px; the book reads as "green book with gold lamp" at 48 px. Distinct from siblings.

## book-2 REJECTED
- Cover shows a regular diagonal diamond / parquet-tile lattice across the whole upper and middle cover (clear in crop-book2.png and at 400 px in c-books-vs-accepted.png). This is the exact lattice defect that failed drink-6 in b5 and is forbidden by the bible (repeated facets, AI tell).
- Rendering is smoother and cooler than book-1/book-3 (the cover reads as printed paper, not gouache); the spine is the only part with brush texture.
- Cover colour is darker and more saturated (about #567790) than the specified #6C8497 / lit #8FA9BC; the lit face should be lighter.
Sparrows (two, simple, brown and cream) and the three wavy lines are good and must be kept.

## book-3 ACCEPTED
Terracotta leather hardback, compass rose without any letters or N/E/S/W, dotted route to a ring mark, dark corner triangles, two gold spine bands. Pose and pitch identical to book-1. Warm key from upper left, shade on the right edge cool. Faint leather mottling is brushy, not a hard lattice. Reads clearly at 48 px (orange book with star). Distinct.

## book-4 REJECTED
- The large flat yellow cover carries a visible diagonal diamond-tile lattice (crop-texture.png right panel, and c-books-vs-accepted.png). Same lattice defect as drink-6 / book-2; on the biggest flat area in the set it is noticed at the 192 px shop size.
- The umbrella is good in concept, but its ribs and tips read as a spiky star-fish at 48 px; the crook handle and canopy should read as one tumbling umbrella.
- Slightly over-saturated yellow (about #FABD2B) against #E8B93D.

## book-5 REJECTED
- Cover blue is far too saturated and cool (about #67A0C8) against the specified pale sky blue #BBD3E0 (shade #8FA9BC); it is the loudest object in the set and is not in the palette. It reads as a different set.
- Gull construction: the body is front-on while the head is in full profile, and the far wing is a detached flap sticking out of the right of the body like a second small wing lobe; legs have odd knee kinks. At the shop size it reads as a lumpy bird.
- Cover is wider than tall by about 1.3 to 1 (brief: squarish, 70 percent tall) and has a visible diagonal streak texture.
The hat sits on the head believably and the thin cream border, cream spine and two red bands are right.

## decor-1 ACCEPTED
Three cushions (amber, teal, brick), stitched seams with a tufted button each, plump believable folds, no bench, no tassels. Fills about 84 percent of the width. Brush is broad and follows the form; light from upper left, shade lower right. Reads as soft cushions at 50 px. Minor notes (not blocking): the "patches" are four quadrants rather than 4 to 6 varied patches, and the brick cushion is slightly orange (coral) rather than deep brick.

## decor-2 ACCEPTED
Bankers lamp: round stepped brass base, slim stem, half-cylinder sage-green shade painted opaque with a lighter band on the upper left, pull chain with bead, brass knobs at the shade ends. No glow, no cone, no bulb. Warm brass lit on the left. Construction is believable (stem enters the shade underside, shade ends close). Silhouette is distinctive at 50 px. Surface is a little smoother than the cups but within the accepted range.

## decor-3 REJECTED
- The terracotta pot is covered in a strong diagonal diamond-tile lattice (crop-texture.png middle panel): the same hard-fail defect as drink-6.
- The foliage is drawn as individual pinnate fronds with a visible central stem and leaflets on both sides, which reads as cypress, juniper or fern, not rosemary, and breaks the brief ("brush masses, not individual needles"). The detail is high-frequency compared with every other accepted asset.
- Violet dots look pasted on rather than painted flowers.
The overall silhouette and the pot proportion are fine.

## decor-4 ACCEPTED
Footed pedestal stand, domed glass cloche with knob, golden fruit tart visible through the dome. Glass is painted opaque in pale blue-green with chalky highlight strokes on the left, shade lower right, soft contact shadow. Reads clearly at 50 px as a cake dome. Notes (not blocking): the tart carries a few raspberries and mint leaves beyond "a few glaze highlights", and the dome highlights are a little blocky.

## decor-5 REJECTED
- Wrong rendering technique: hard, glossy, CG-render look with dark grey metal wheels and a noisy wood grain, not matte gouache; it looks like it came from another set when put next to the accepted objects (c-decor-vs-accepted.png).
- Construction and perspective: the whole ladder is rotated in the picture plane (left foot at about y=820, right foot at about y=890), so it does not stand on anything and the contact shadow is detached; the left rail is much narrower than the right rail, so the rails do not read as parallel and equal; it should lean BACK about 8 degrees (top narrower), not sideways.
- Wheels are dark grey, not brass; the brass rail overhang is fine.
- Rung count (six) and brass foot caps are correct and should be kept.
